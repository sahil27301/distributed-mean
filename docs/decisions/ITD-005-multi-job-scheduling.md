| ITD 5 - *Use round robin across running jobs, ordered by last claim time* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | When a worker is free and several jobs are running, which job does it claim from? |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | ***Round robin: pick the running job with the oldest `last_claimed_at` (initialised to the time the job becomes `running`) that has ≥2 claimable items, using `SKIP LOCKED`*** <br> FIFO by job <br> Shortest remaining work first <br> Priority per job / customer <br> Fair share weighted by work (files × C) <br> Round robin over only the K oldest jobs |
| **REASONING** | No job waits behind another (in the inspiration: no company waits for another). New jobs start within one cycle, and no job starves. <br> Stateless: the job row lock is held only inside the short claim transaction, so no semaphore, no TTL, and crashes release locks automatically. Concurrent claims skip locked jobs and naturally take the next one. <br> With the two indexes, a claim checks at most W jobs and ~6W rows. <br> FIFO blocks small jobs behind huge ones. Shortest-first can starve large jobs. Priority and work-weighted fairness are not required yet. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | Fair by claims, not by work: a job with C = 1M uses ~1000× more worker time per claim than one with C = 1k. <br> With many equal jobs, all progress slowly and finish late; FIFO would give a lower average completion time. <br> A job's final merge waits roughly (active jobs ÷ W) task durations. |
| **NOTES** | `last_claimed_at` is updated at claim time, inside the claim transaction; updating it after processing would let every free worker pile onto the same job until the first one finishes. If the claiming worker crashes, the job has lost its turn and waits at most one cycle plus one lease (≤30 s) for its items to return, which is acceptable since crashes are rare. <br> K-oldest round robin and work-weighted fairness are the natural next steps. |
